Research Overview

Thanh Đàm Cổ Ngoạn is devoted to the study of Vietnamese art, antiquities, iconography, and material culture. The research presented on this platform focuses on the historical development of Vietnamese visual traditions and their relationship to religion, politics, craftsmanship, and cultural exchange.

VI: Thanh Đàm Cổ Ngoạn hướng tới việc nghiên cứu mỹ thuật, cổ vật, hình tượng học và văn hóa vật thể Việt Nam, tập trung vào sự phát triển của các truyền thống tạo hình Việt Nam trong mối quan hệ với tín ngưỡng, chính trị, kỹ thuật chế tác và giao lưu văn hóa.

Current Research Themes

Vietnamese Dragon Iconography

The historical evolution of dragon imagery from Đông Sơn through the Nguyễn dynasty, including imperial, religious, and architectural contexts.

Nghê and Guardian Creatures

The role of the nghê in temples, communal houses, palaces, and traditional Vietnamese architecture, and its distinction from Chinese and East Asian guardian beasts.

Buddhist Donor Portraits (Hậu Phật)

Iconography, social history, and religious patronage associated with donor portraits in Vietnamese Buddhist sculpture and temple art.

Imperial and Commissioned Porcelain

The cultural and historical significance of Vietnamese commissioned ceramics and the broader context of regional ceramic exchange.

Han-Nom Inscriptions

The relationship between inscriptions, iconography, and historical documentation in Vietnamese antiquities and ritual objects.

Traditional Decorative Motifs

Lotus, chrysanthemum, cloud, wave, mountain, and geometric patterns as carriers of symbolic meaning across Vietnamese artistic traditions.

Methodology

The research combines iconographic analysis, art historical interpretation, material culture studies, and comparative East Asian scholarship. Objects are examined not only as works of art but also as historical documents that preserve information about belief systems, political authority, craftsmanship, trade, and cultural identity.

Research Statement

“Vietnamese art has continuously absorbed, transformed, and reinterpreted external influences while maintaining a distinct cultural identity throughout the historical development of Đại Việt.”

The long-term objective of this project is to contribute to the international understanding of Vietnamese civilization through rigorous documentation, bilingual publication, and the study of historical artifacts and visual culture.
